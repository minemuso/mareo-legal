Privacy Policy

Last updated: September 8, 2026

Introduction

Mareo does not collect your personal data. This Privacy Policy exists to say that precisely, and to describe what the App does with the information you enter — all of which stays on your device.

Wherever you are, data-protection law protects information that is collected about you. This App collects none, and the pages below explain exactly what it does instead.

Who Is Responsible for Your Data

Mareo is published by Mustafa Erol, Türkiye.

This version of the App has no account, no server and no analytics. The records you enter are written to storage on your own device and are never transmitted to us or to anyone else. We do not receive them, we cannot read them, and we could not produce, correct or delete them on request — there would be nothing on our side to look up. We therefore hold no personal data about you at all.

That is stated plainly rather than left to be inferred, because naming a data controller for information that never reaches them describes a relationship that does not exist.

For any question about this Policy or about the App, write to mareo.app@gmail.com.

What the App Stores, and Where

Nothing is collected. This section describes what the App writes to your device.

  • Records you enter: the aircraft, components, schedules, maintenance and flight logs, defect records, notes, photos and documents you create. They are written to this device's storage only.
  • Names and signatures you enter: the technician or inspector names you record against work, and the signature image you save for log cards. They are yours to enter and yours to remove.
  • Supplier contact details, if you add any: a company name, contact person, e-mail address, telephone number and address. These belong to other people, so they are worth calling out separately — they stay on this device exactly like everything else, and we never receive them.
  • Reminders: if you turn them on, they are scheduled by iOS on this device. No push token is created and nothing is registered with any server.
  • No account. There is no sign-in, so there is no account e-mail address or password to hold.
  • No usage analytics. Nothing about how you use the App is sent anywhere — there is nowhere to send it. A little is kept on the device so the App can behave sensibly: which getting-started steps you have finished, and what you have dismissed or snoozed on the planner. That never leaves this device, and it goes when the App does.
  • No advertising or tracking identifiers. The App does not read the advertising identifier and does not track you across other apps or websites.

How Your Information Is Used

It is used to run the App on your device: to show your fleet, to work out what is due and when, to draw the reports and log cards you export, and to schedule the reminders you ask for.

All of that happens on the device. Nothing is sent anywhere, so there is nothing for us to use.

Where Your Data Lives

Your records sit in the App's own storage on this device, inside the sandbox the operating system gives every app, behind whatever passcode or biometric lock you have set on the device itself.

Most of what the App stores is written encrypted: your aircraft, components, maintenance and flight records, defect records, notes, suppliers, and the index of your photos and documents. Some of it is not, and rather than describe that as "display preferences" it is worth naming — the settings you save for log cards (technician name, organisation name and signature image), your list of past reminders, and your quality reports are written in plain form.

The photos and documents themselves are ordinary files in the App's own folder and are not encrypted. The encryption covers the record of them, not the image — so a scan of a certificate is stored the way any photo on your phone is stored.

Because nothing is transmitted, the risks that come with transmission do not arise here. The risk that does remain is the device: anyone who can unlock your phone can open the App. Keep a passcode on it — and keep your own exports, because they are the only copy that survives losing it.

One thing about those exports: the file is plain readable text, not encrypted. That is deliberate — it means you can open it, check it, and restore it anywhere without depending on us — but it also means anyone who gets the file can read everything in it. Keep it somewhere you control, and think before sending it to anyone.

Third-Party Services

The App is distributed through Apple's App Store, and Apple's own terms cover that distribution and any download record Apple keeps of it.

Beyond that, the App uses no third-party service: no analytics provider, no crash reporter, no advertising network, no cloud backend.

Deleting Your Data

There is no account to delete. Your data is on your device and you control it directly: delete individual records inside the App, or delete everything at once by deleting the App — iOS removes its storage along with it.

Three things deletion does not reach, stated plainly rather than left to be discovered:

  • Any copy you exported. Once the App has handed a file to the share sheet it never learns where the file went, or whether it was kept, so deleting the App cannot reach it and does not go looking for it.
  • Part of a copy inside your own device backup — and which part is worth knowing, because it is not the part most people assume. The App's working storage is marked as excluded from iCloud and computer backups, so the live copy of your aircraft, components, maintenance, logs and notes is not in one. What IS in a device backup are the App's files: the photos and documents it keeps in its own folder, and — if you have ever used Export Data — up to three copies of the export file itself, which the App keeps so it can offer your fleet back if the App ever comes up empty. Those export copies are plain readable text and they do contain your records. You can see how many there are and delete them under Settings > Data Management. Restore a device backup onto a new phone and the App opens empty of live records, with those files still on disk. That backup is yours and is governed by Apple's terms; we have no access to it. Your own export file is still the only thing that puts the records back, which is the reason to keep one.
  • The encryption key itself. iOS does not remove keychain entries when an app is deleted, so that key remains until the device is erased. On its own it reveals nothing: it opens only this App's storage, which deletion has already removed.

Your Rights

Data-protection law gives you rights against whoever holds your personal data: to learn whether it is processed, to see it, to have it corrected or erased, and to have any correction passed on. Those rights follow the data.

We hold none of it. There is nothing for us to disclose, correct, erase or pass on, and a request to us could not produce your records, because we would have nothing to look up.

What you can do immediately, without asking anyone: export your records (Settings > Export Data), and delete everything by deleting the App.

Know what the export is before you rely on it. It carries the records you enter — aircraft, components, maintenance tasks, flight, maintenance and defect logs, the parts shelf, and notes. It does NOT put back: aircraft cover photos; your notification history; the photo and document files themselves; and the record of every change you saved. Two more travel in the file and are not applied on restore: the record of your photos and documents; and the activity history, with its comments and tasks — and the restore screen says so before it runs. Treat the export as a copy of your maintenance history rather than of the whole App.

Data Retention

We keep nothing, so we have no retention period to describe.

On your device, your records stay until you delete them, with three exceptions worth naming rather than leaving to be discovered:

  • Notes you move to the trash are removed permanently thirty days later.
  • The part-change history is kept up to a fixed size rather than a fixed number of entries. Once it is full, the oldest are dropped as newer ones are written. The Export Data screen shows how many entries this device is holding, so the limit is a number you can read rather than one you discover.
  • The activity history, and the comments in it, are kept the same way and up to a size of their own.
  • The change log — the record of which field you changed, and what it was before — is kept up to a limit YOU set in Settings. When it reaches that limit nothing is deleted: the older entries are set aside on this device and stay readable and exportable. The change log screen tells you when this has happened, and it is the only place the whole history can be ended — which only you can do, deliberately, after taking a complete copy.

Nothing else expires and nothing else is removed on a schedule.
